# Heath

> Sleep, unbroken. Heath is a sleep wearable in development. It follows your sleep through the night and settles the restless moments, so you get the most restorative sleep and recover best.

Status: in development. Not for sale. No price and no release date announced. The form of the device has not been announced.

## The problem it works on

Every night, sleep turns restless many times. Most of these moments are not enough to wake you, but they cost you the restorative sleep you went to bed for. Healthy sleepers stir about 11 times an hour in their twenties and about 22 by their fifties (Bonnet & Arand, J Clin Sleep Med, 2007).

## How it works

- Feel: it follows your sleep, all night.
- Notice: it catches the moment sleep starts to thin.
- Settle: a little warmth, a low tone or a light touch. Then quiet.

## The three signals (planned)

- Warmth: warming the skin by 0.4 C deepened sleep; in older adults, early waking fell from 58% to 4% (Raymann et al., Brain, 2008).
- Touch: slow rocking cut arousals in deep sleep by 60% (Perrault et al., Current Biology, 2019).
- Sound: soft tones in rhythm with slow waves made them stronger and longer (Ngo et al., Neuron, 2013).

These studies tested the signals in sleep labs. They did not test Heath. Heath's own results have not been published.

## Why it matters

- Older adults with the most fragmented sleep were 1.5 times as likely to develop Alzheimer's (Lim et al., Sleep, 2013).
- Deep sleep is about a fifth of the night before 25; after 35 it falls under 5%, in a study of 149 healthy men (Van Cauter et al., JAMA, 2000).

## Principles

1. Recovery is the point. Sleep is not a score to chase.
2. Act in the moment. Settle a restless night while it is happening, rather than report it the next morning.
3. Stay out of sight. If you notice Heath, it has done something wrong.
4. Say only what is known. No miracle claims, no invented numbers.
5. Sleep first. Better nights, then better days.
